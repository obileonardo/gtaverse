# Can GTAverse use Rockstar's official assets? Legal research for a GTA VI fan site

Research date: 23 November 2026. Researcher: Research Agent B.
**This is research, not legal advice.** It is written so the owner can make a product decision and know where the real risk sits. Before monetizing, or if a takedown or demand letter arrives, talk to an IP lawyer (US-qualified for DMCA and trademark questions).

Labels used below:
- **Verified**: read on the primary source on the date above, quoted exactly.
- **Reported**: from press or third-party sites, not confirmed by Rockstar or Take-Two.
- **Inference**: my reasoning from the verified material.
- **Unknown**: could not be established.

---

## 1. Short answer

1. **There is no fan kit and no brand-asset licence.** Rockstar and Take-Two publish no fan content licence, fan site kit, logo guidelines or press-asset terms that grant fans rights. Searches found none, and the GTA VI media and downloads pages carry no licence text (verified, see 2.4).
2. **There is a written tolerance policy, and it names fan sites.** Rockstar's support article "Policy on posting copyrighted Rockstar Games material" asks, in its own words, "Can I use copyrighted material on fan sites if it is for non-commercial use and doesn't have spoilers?" and answers: "Generally, Take-Two Interactive does not object to our fans using materials for non-commercial uses in a manner which does not intentionally spoil the plot for others." (verified, see 2.1). That is a tolerance, revocable "at any time", not a licence.
3. **Copyright material (screenshots, key art, the official map, clips) is usable under that tolerance while the site is non-commercial, with credit and no spoilers.** This is exactly what the thriving GTA VI fan maps do today (see section 4).
4. **Trademarks are the line not to cross.** The GTA VI logo, the Rockstar "R*" logo, the "GTA" mark used as your brand, and a look that makes people think the site is official are the highest-risk choices. Rockstar's own Mod Guidelines say: "Do not use Rockstar Games IP or logos to promote or otherwise suggest that your mod or custom server is endorsed by or affiliated with Rockstar Games." (verified). That sentence is written for mods, but it is the clearest statement of what Rockstar objects to.
5. **The official GTA VI typeface is proprietary.** The GTA VI website serves its own font family named "GTAArtDeco" (verified in the page source, see 3f). It is not licensed to anyone. Do not extract or self-host it.
6. **Ads change the picture.** The tolerance is explicitly for "non-commercial" use. Ads move the site outside the written tolerance and into fair-use territory (editorial use only) or into needing a licence. Competitors do run ads and paid tiers with official art and are not being taken down, but that is Rockstar's discretion, not a right.

**Recommendation (ranked, detail in section 6):**
1. **Original brand, official media used editorially.** Own name, own wordmark, own fonts, own icons; official screenshots, key art and the official Leonida map used inside content with "© Rockstar Games" credit; clips embedded, never re-hosted. Best balance of "feels official" and survivability. **Recommended.**
2. **Fully original visuals.** Own illustrated map and no official imagery at all. Safest, weakest "official" feel, much more work.
3. **Near-identical clone of the official site** (GTA VI logo, Rockstar logo, GTA Art Deco font, the rockstargames.com/VI layout). Not recommended: it is the one route that invites a trademark complaint, and it gets worse the moment ads arrive.

## 2. What Rockstar and Take-Two actually publish

### 2.1 Policy on posting copyrighted Rockstar Games material (the key document)

Source: https://support.rockstargames.com/en-US/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz (redirects to https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-rockstar-games-material). Search snippets report a "last updated" date of 10 January 2025; I could not see the date in the fetched text (Unknown). The page blocks automated fetching; the text below was retrieved through a reader proxy and quoted verbatim.

Exact clauses that matter:

> "**Question:** Am I allowed to post game footage on YouTube or other websites? Can I use copyrighted material on fan sites if it is for non-commercial use and doesn't have spoilers?"
>
> "**Answer:** Generally, Take-Two Interactive does not object to our fans using materials for non-commercial uses in a manner which does not intentionally spoil the plot for others."
>
> "'Non-commercial' means that you don't make money through the game footage you post or use the material as part of a promotion for a product or service. Take-Two Interactive reserves the right to have its copyrighted material taken down at any time."

Takedown triggers it lists (each quoted as headings, with key lines):

> "Pre-Release Footage: No pre-release leaked footage of any kind. Any posting of in-game footage from leaked copies of the game prior to its official release date will be taken down, regardless of how the game was obtained."
>
> "Spoilers: Posting video showing the ending of the game, an edited combination of cutscenes and/or in-game plot dialogue, or any other big reveal in the story. It's ok to show cutscenes as part of a larger play-through (or in a narrated 'Let's Play' type video) but isolated videos of the game's cinematics will be removed."
>
> "In-Game Entertainment: Showing content like the TV shows and comedy performances in Grand Theft Auto, or the silent movies in Red Dead Redemption. We prefer fans to experience these in the context of the game's world, so any posting of these in isolation will be flagged for removal."
>
> "Encouraging Cheating" (exploits, money glitches, mod menus in online titles).
>
> "Promoting Modifications / Ports: distributing or promoting unauthorized mods or ports that violate Rockstar's rights."

Scope note at the end:

> "Please note: this policy is intended to address occasional, non-commercial in-game content use by individual members of the Rockstar fan community. It does not apply to the exploitation of Rockstar IP for film, TV, or streaming distribution; in music videos; advertisements; or publication in books, magazines, or digital publishing. ... Licensing requests can be submitted to copyright@take2games.com for case-by-case consideration; requests must include a detailed description of the content in question and specific proposed use in order to be eligible for review."

And: "This is not meant to be an exhaustive statement of Take-Two's policy, and we reserve the right to remove content we find objectionable on a case-by-case basis."

**What this means for GTAverse (inference):**
- A free, non-commercial fan site is squarely the case the policy describes. This is the strongest document in the owner's favour.
- "Digital publishing" is excluded from the policy's scope. A guide website could be argued to be "digital publishing". The safer reading: the policy covers a fan site while it is genuinely fan-run and non-commercial; a commercial publication is outside it.
- Discoveries that are story spoilers, isolated cutscenes, or isolated in-game TV, radio comedy or cartoon segments should not be embedded as standalone clips. Those are named takedown targets.
- Anything from leaks (including pre-release map leaks and the September 2026 "archive" material) is excluded absolutely.

### 2.2 Rockstar Games Terms of Service

Source: https://www.rockstargames.com/legal ("Last Updated: February 28, 2025", verified). The owner, as a player with a Rockstar account, is bound by these.

> 2.1: Rockstar owns "all ... designs, graphics, artwork, illustrations, photographs, ... titles, themes, objects, characters, names, dialogue, locations, stories, plot ... and (2) trademarks, logos, trade names, trade dress, service marks, and trade identities".
>
> 2.3: "you may not ... copy ... distribute ... modify, create derivative works, commercialize, or otherwise exploit the Services (including the Content) ... unless subject to separate, express written terms provided by Rockstar permitting such conduct. ... nothing in the limited license granted in this Agreement authorizes the use of the Services (including the Content) ... in any manner to develop, train, enhance, or provide source material for, or promote, any Generative AI Tools; and any such uses are hereby explicitly prohibited."
>
> 5.3: "Custom Content includes, without limitation, all content created using Our Tools including in-game assets, maps, screenshots, videos, recordings of in-game audio, gameplay clips, and livestreams. You may only use Custom Content with the Services and/or only as authorized by us."
>
> 5.4: "Rockstar reserves all rights to and ownership of all Custom Content under applicable law. If ... your creation of Custom Content results in you holding any intellectual property rights ... you hereby freely assign us ... all right, title, and interest".
>
> 17.5(9): copyright and trademark claims are excluded from arbitration and can go to court; governing law is New York (section 17.1).

**What this means (inference):**
- Rockstar claims ownership of every player screenshot and clip. A YouTuber cannot give GTAverse rights to their GTA clip; only Rockstar can. Crediting finders is courtesy and community practice, not a licence.
- The "authorized by us" route for player captures is the posting policy in 2.1. That is why that policy matters so much.
- **Generative AI warning:** feeding Rockstar content (screenshots, map, art) into AI image or video tools to make site art is "explicitly prohibited" for anyone bound by the ToS. At least one competitor shows "AI-animated from official Rockstar Games artwork" on its homepage (theleonidamap.com, observed 23 Nov 2026). Do not copy that.

### 2.3 Mod Guidelines (10 September 2026)

Source: https://www.rockstargames.com/community-resources/mod-guidelines (dated "September 10, 2026", verified). These govern mods, not websites, but they are Rockstar's most recent statement of what it protects:

> "Do not use Rockstar Games IP or logos to promote or otherwise suggest that your mod or custom server is endorsed by or affiliated with Rockstar Games."
>
> "Do not make mods that combine maps, locations, characters, storylines, in-game assets, or other IP from one Rockstar Games title into another title."
>
> "Unless permitted via Rockstar Games' official Creator Platform, all modding must be strictly non-commercial."
>
> "These Mod Guidelines Are Not a License. ... Nothing in these guidelines waives or limits the right of Rockstar Games or Take-Two Interactive to enforce its intellectual property".

Inference: the cross-game lore pages are commentary, not a mod, so the "combine IP" rule does not apply. But a page that composites RDR2 and GTA VI art into new images edges toward the kind of mash-up Rockstar dislikes. Keep cross-game pages to side-by-side editorial comparison.

### 2.4 Official GTA VI media and downloads pages

Sources: https://www.rockstargames.com/VI/media and https://www.rockstargames.com/VI/downloads (verified 23 Nov 2026).

- Rockstar offers zip downloads: 138 screenshots, 9 video clips, 28 artwork and wallpapers, 51 Ultimate Edition images, 12 Vintage Vice City Pack images, and a "Leonida Map & Cover Art Poster": "Get print-ready formats of the official game map and cover art poster."
- No licence or usage terms appear on either page. The footer links only to the general Legal page.
- Reported (Leonida Verse, 19 Nov 2026, https://leonidaverse.com/en/news/gta-6-official-leonida-map-printable-rockstar): the map is a 5,775 x 7,900 px, 300 dpi file titled "The Great State of Leonida", with five counties (Lummox, Kelly, Leonard, Vice-Dale, Mariana) and an A to M, 1 to 9 grid.

Inference: publicly offered downloads are an invitation to fans to use them for personal and fan purposes, which fits the 2.1 policy. They are not a licence for commercial use or branding.

### 2.5 What does not exist (searched, not found)

- No Rockstar or Take-Two "fan content policy" in the style of Riot, Microsoft or Wizards of the Coast; no fan site kit; no logo or brand usage guidelines for the public; no public press-site terms. (Unknown whether a gated press portal has terms: none were reachable.)
- Rockstar's Newswire and social pages do not grant reuse rights.

## 3. Asset-by-asset verdicts

Scale: **allowed** / **allowed with conditions** / **risky** / **not allowed**.

### a. GTA VI logo and Rockstar logo
**Verdict: not allowed as branding; risky even editorially.**
- Both are registered trademarks and copyrighted artwork (ToS 2.1 names "trademarks, logos").
- Using either in the site header, favicon, social cards or as a "badge" implies endorsement, which is what the Mod Guidelines forbid and what trademark law treats as confusion.
- Plain-text references ("Grand Theft Auto VI", "Rockstar Games") are fine (nominative use, see i).
- If a news article shows official key art that happens to include the logo (e.g. the cover art), that is the official image used editorially, which is lower risk than placing the logo yourself.

### b. Official key art, trailer screenshots, loading-screen art
**Verdict: allowed with conditions.**
- Conditions: non-commercial site; credit "Image: Rockstar Games"; use inside content (articles, discovery cards, lore pages), not as the site's brand or chrome; no edits that misrepresent; no AI processing (ToS 2.3); not from leaks.
- Basis: the 2.1 policy plus Rockstar offering these as downloads. Competitors do exactly this with visible credit lines: Grand Leonida shows "Images © Rockstar Games, shown for editorial reference"; The Leonida Map shows "Official imagery belongs to Rockstar Games and is used for informational purposes" (both observed 23 Nov 2026).
- Loading-screen art captured from the game is Custom Content (ToS 5.3) and sits in the same bucket.
- With ads: drops to **risky** outside news and commentary use (section 3j).

### c. In-game screenshots and clips captured by players
**Verdict: allowed with conditions.**
- Rockstar owns them (ToS 5.4) and tolerates non-commercial fan use (2.1).
- Conditions: no story spoilers or isolated cutscenes, no isolated in-game TV or comedy segments, nothing showing cheats, mod menus or glitch money exploits, nothing pre-release.
- GTAverse's own captures: fine under the same conditions. Third-party captures: embed them (h) or ask the creator; do not download and re-host someone else's capture.

### d. The in-game map image, and tracing it into a vector map
**Verdict: allowed with conditions (official map as base layer); tracing is not legally safer.**
- The official Leonida map is a Rockstar artwork offered as a download (2.4). MapGenie titles its page "Official Grand Theft Auto 6 Map" (https://mapgenie.io/grand-theft-auto-6/maps/leonida, observed) and Leonida Verse says its interactive map "now shows this official map". This is the industry norm and Rockstar has not objected.
- **Tracing does not escape copyright.** Leonida is a fictional place; its coastline, road layout and labels are Rockstar's creative expression, not real-world facts. A vector trace is a derivative work of the same artwork (ToS 2.3 forbids "create derivative works"). It looks more "yours" but carries the same legal status. Inference, high confidence.
- What a trace does buy: control over styling, file size, and a map that does not look like a copied image. It also makes the site depend on Rockstar's tolerance in exactly the same way.
- Capturing the in-game pause map and stitching it: Custom Content, same tolerance, same conditions.
- Recommendation: use the official map image as a tiled base layer with a visible "Map © Rockstar Games" attribution; keep the tile pipeline swappable so a takedown can be met by switching to an original illustrated map.
- Do not use leaked or "early archive" maps. Take-Two filed DMCA takedowns over early GTA VI map images in September 2026 (reported, https://ixbt.games/en/news/2026/09/22/take-two-pytaetsia-udalit-iz-interneta-kadry-dopolnenii-dlia-gta-v-i-ranniuiu-kartu-gta-vi.html).

### e. HUD and minimap blip icons recreated near-identically
**Verdict: risky; prefer original icons.**
- Blips are small Rockstar artworks. Near-identical recreations are copies of protected expression; tiny simple shapes have thin protection, but a whole matched set reads as copied UI and as trade dress.
- Observed: GTABase serves files named "game-blip-*.webp" on its GTA VI map (https://www.gtabase.com/gta-6/map/), so the practice is tolerated today.
- Better: an original icon set that reads clearly at map scale. The owner's standing rule (all projects use the Capsa icon style) already points this way and lowers risk at the same time.

### f. Typefaces
**Verdict: GTA VI site font not allowed; Pricedown allowed only with the right licence and is the wrong era; use a licensed alternative.**
- **GTA VI website font (verified):** the page source of https://www.rockstargames.com/VI loads font files named `GTAArtDeco_Regular`, `GTAArtDeco_Medium`, `GTAArtDeco_Bold`, `GTAArtDeco_CondensedBold` and `GTAArtDeco_CondensedHeavy`. It is a custom, proprietary family. Downloading or self-hosting it is copyright infringement of the font software and an obvious "copied their site" signal. Not allowed.
- **GTA VI logo lettering:** custom-drawn artwork (part of the trademark). No public typeface reproduces it officially. Unknown whether it derives from a commercial face. Do not imitate it closely.
- **Pricedown:** the classic 3D-era GTA logo face, designed by Ray Larabie (Typodermic), based on the logo of The Price Is Right (GTA Wiki, https://gta.fandom.com/wiki/Fonts). Licensing is mixed in what I found: older Larabie freeware terms allowed free commercial use; current Typodermic desktop terms (as described on listing sites) say webfonts, apps and SaaS need a separate licence (https://www.fontsquirrel.com/license/Pricedown, https://myfonts.com/fonts/typodermic/pricedown). For a website that serves the font as a webfont, assume a paid webfont licence is needed and confirm on typodermicfonts.com. It is also the GTA III to V look, not the GTA VI look.
- Recommendation: choose a properly licensed display face (open-licence or bought webfont) that evokes the era without copying the GTA Art Deco family. The owner picks the face.

### g. Colour palette and layout mimicry (trade dress)
**Verdict: mood is allowed; a combined replica is risky.**
- Colours alone (the sunset pink, orange and teal of Miami and Vice City) are not protectable and are generic to the setting.
- Trade dress and passing-off risk grows when several signals combine: logo-like wordmark + Rockstar's layout and navigation + the GTA VI font + "official" wording. The test is whether a visitor could think the site is Rockstar's.
- Practical rule: borrow the era and the place, never the official site's specific composition (hero layout, nav pattern, card styling, logo lockup).

### h. Embedding YouTube, X, TikTok and Reddit clips
**Verdict: allowed with conditions; creator permission not legally required for platform embeds.**
- **YouTube (verified, https://www.youtube.com/t/terms):** "You may also show YouTube videos through the embeddable YouTube player." Uploaders grant "each other user of the Service a worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free license to access your Content through the Service, and to use that Content ... only as enabled by a feature of the Service (such as video playback or embeds)."
- **X (verified, https://x.com/en/tos):** users grant X a licence that "authorizes us to make your Content available to the rest of the world and to let others do the same." Use X's official embed.
- **Reddit (verified, https://redditinc.com/policies/embeds-terms):** embeds are licensed, but "Nothing in these Terms shall be deemed to grant you any right, title, or interest in User Content. You are solely responsible for using User Content in compliance with any requirements or restrictions imposed by applicable rightsholders." Also: "Do not modify, alter, obscure, or disable any elements ... made visible on your App through Reddit Embeds" and "Do not use Reddit Embeds to display User Content in advertisements".
- **TikTok (verified page, https://www.tiktok.com/embed):** offers official embeds for sites. I did not find embed-specific terms text beyond the general terms (Unknown detail).
- **US case law:** the Ninth Circuit's "server test" says embedding is not a direct "display" by the embedding site (Hunley v. Instagram, 9th Cir. 17 July 2023, https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/22-15293/22-15293-2023-07-17.html). Some New York district courts rejected that test (Goldman v. Breitbart, S.D.N.Y. 2018, reported in https://www.dwt.com/insights/2023/08/server-test-instagram-case-ninth-circuit). Platform licences are the stronger footing.
- Conditions: use the official embed players only; never download and re-host; do not crop out the creator's name or the platform chrome; credit the finder in text with a link; honour embedding being disabled; remove on the creator's request; do not embed spoiler or isolated-cutscene clips (Rockstar may take those down anyway, which breaks the embed).
- Inference: asking creators for permission is good community practice and a differentiator ("featured with permission"), not a legal requirement.

### i. Using "GTA" or "Grand Theft Auto" in the site name or domain
**Verdict: in descriptive text, allowed; as the brand name ("GTAverse"), risky.**
- "GTA" and "Grand Theft Auto" are Take-Two trademarks. Nominative use (referring to the game to describe what the site covers) is lawful in the US when you use only as much of the mark as needed and do not suggest sponsorship (the New Kids on the Block test; applied to domain names in Toyota v. Tabari, 9th Cir. 2010). Inference from settled doctrine; no fetched source.
- A brand that fuses the mark ("GTA" + "verse") reads like an official product line and is harder to defend than "a fan guide to GTA VI".
- Domains: UDRP panels have repeatedly transferred GTA-based domains to Take-Two (example: gta5betagiveaway.net, WIPO D2013-0785, https://www.wipo.int/amc/en/domains/decisions/text/2013/d2013-0785.html, a phishing case). WIPO's Overview 3.0 section 2.7 says a fan site needs to be "active, genuinely noncommercial, and clearly distinct from any official complainant site", and that mark-plus-term names (like `<celebrity-fan.tld>`) can be fair if non-commercial, with "a limited degree of incidental commercial activity" sometimes tolerated (https://www.wipo.int/amc/en/domains/search/overview3.0/, verified).
- **The personal subdomain helps:** UDRP covers registered domain names, so a subdomain of the owner's personal domain is not a UDRP target (inference). A trademark complaint to the host is still possible.
- **Name collision (verified 23 Nov 2026):** `gtaverse.com` is parked for sale at US$300,000; `gtaverse.net` is a Turkish GTA forum titled "GTAVerse"; a direct competitor is called "Leonida Verse" (leonidaverse.com). The "-verse" naming is crowded.
- Recommendation: a brand name without "GTA", with a descriptor such as "an unofficial guide to Grand Theft Auto VI and the Rockstar universe". If the owner keeps "GTAverse", never stylise "GTA" like the official logo and always pair it with "unofficial fan site".

### j. Monetization later (ads) and how it changes risk
**Verdict: allowed with conditions for an original-content site; risky for official imagery used decoratively.**
- The written tolerance (2.1) is limited to non-commercial use, defined as not making "money through the game footage you post" and not using the material "as part of a promotion for a product or service". Ads around Rockstar images fall outside it.
- What remains with ads: US fair use for genuine news, commentary and guide content (transformative, small amounts, credited). Decorative or brand use of official art does not have that defence.
- Observed reality (23 Nov 2026): ad-supported and paid sites use official imagery and maps widely: MapGenie ("an IGN Entertainment Inc. brand", official map), Game8 (accounts, "Premium Articles"), GTABase (memberships, and a contributor contest paying "$100 of Rockstar Store merch"), Rockstar Intel (news with official art). None has been reported as taken down for that. Inference: Take-Two tolerates commercial guide sites because they promote the game, but it reserves the right to change its mind, and a small site with no relationship has less leverage than IGN.
- WIPO's fan-site guidance treats "incidental commercial activity" to cover costs more kindly than a profit site (2.7.3).
- Before turning on ads: (1) remove official art from site chrome, social cards and OG images (use original art); (2) keep official images only inside editorial content with credit; (3) do not run ads on pages built mainly around a single official image or clip; (4) consider a licence request to copyright@take2games.com, which the policy explicitly invites ("for case-by-case consideration").

## 5. Jurisdiction notes

- **Owner in Nigeria.** Nigeria's Copyright Act 2022 has a fair-dealing exception (section 20, open-ended "such as" purposes) and a notice-and-takedown regime for online service providers (Part VII). Sources: https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/copyright/1327992/intellectual-property-law-in-nigeria-the-copyright-act-2022 and the official text at https://www.copyright.gov.ng/CopyrightAct/CopyrightAct2023FinalPublication1.pdf (not opened; section numbers beyond 20 unverified).
- **Hosted on Cloudflare (US).** US copyright law and the DMCA notice system (17 U.S.C. 512) apply in practice: Take-Two's notices go to Cloudflare, which acts on content it hosts (Pages, Workers, R2) and has a repeat-infringer policy. Treat US fair use as the operative test.
- **ToS forum.** Rockstar's ToS are governed by New York law, and copyright and trademark claims are carved out of arbitration (17.5(9)), so Take-Two could sue in a US court. Realistically, against a small non-commercial fan site, the first step is a DMCA notice, not a lawsuit (inference from the precedents above).
- **Account risk.** ToS section 6 lets Rockstar take "Adverse Action" (including account suspension) for breaches. Unlikely for a fan site, but it is the owner's Rockstar account on the line, which is one more reason to stay inside the posting policy.
- **Personal subdomain.** A takedown aimed at GTAverse could affect the hosting account behind the owner's personal domain. Inference: host GTAverse as its own Cloudflare project (and ideally its own zone) so any action stays contained.

## 6. Recommendation: official assets vs near-identical inspired design

### Option 1 (recommended): original brand, official media used editorially
- Brand: own name (ideally without "GTA"), own wordmark, licensed display font, own icon set, own colours chosen by the owner. Feels like the era, never like the official site.
- Content: official screenshots, key art and the official Leonida map used inside content with "© Rockstar Games" credit; embedded clips from platform players with finder credit.
- Guardrails: no logos, no leaks, spoiler gating, no AI processing of Rockstar content, clear disclaimer.
- Risk: low while non-commercial; manageable with ads if official art stays editorial.
- Why first: it matches what every surviving competitor does, it is what the 2.1 policy describes, and it keeps the "official look" where users feel it most (real game imagery and the real map) while the brand stays defensible.

### Option 2: fully original visuals
- Own illustrated Leonida map (drawn by GTAverse, not traced), own art, no official imagery.
- Risk: lowest on copyright for imagery; the map is still a depiction of Rockstar's fictional geography, so "lowest", not "zero".
- Cost: large art effort; less "official" feel; the map lags behind discoveries.
- Use it as the fallback layer for Option 1.

### Option 3 (not recommended): near-identical clone of rockstargames.com/VI
- GTA VI logo and R* logo in the header, GTA Art Deco font, matching layout and copy tone.
- Risk: high. Trademark confusion and font infringement are the claims most likely to succeed; Rockstar's own guidelines name "suggest ... endorsed by or affiliated with" as the thing it acts on; it gets worse with ads and is the most likely to be removed in one notice.

## 8. Takedown-response process (recommended)

1. **Publish a contact route** (email form on a dedicated page) for copyright and trademark concerns. Respond within 24 hours.
2. **On any notice from Take-Two, Rockstar or their agents:** remove or disable the named item at once, without arguing first. Keep a private log: date, sender, item, URL, action taken.
3. **Do not file counter-notices against Take-Two** unless a lawyer advises it. A counter-notice invites a lawsuit in the US.
4. **Check for a pattern.** If the notice targets a category (e.g. official map tiles), switch that category to the original fallback (Option 2 layer) site-wide.
5. **Creator requests** (a YouTuber wants their clip removed or wants different credit): honour within 24 hours, no questions.
6. **Impersonation or name complaints** (e.g. about "GTA" in the name): have a rename plan ready; keep the brand in one config so a rename is a quick change.
7. **Cloudflare notices:** reply through Cloudflare's process; keep GTAverse on its own project or zone so action does not touch other sites.
8. **Pre-emptive hygiene:** a "no leaks" rule for contributors; source labels on every pin (in-game verified, official media, community report); never accept submissions from leak material.
